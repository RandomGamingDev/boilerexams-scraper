This is a standard economic supply and demand graph. Below is an exhaustive description of its components:

### 1. Axes and Labels
*   **Vertical Axis (y-axis):** Labeled **$P$** at the top.
*   **Horizontal Axis (x-axis):** Labeled **$Q$** at the bottom right.
*   **Intersection:** The axes meet at an origin $(0,0)$.

### 2. Curves
*   **Demand Curve ($D$):** A downward-sloping straight line. It intersects the vertical axis at $P = 50$.
*   **Original Supply Curve ($S$):** An upward-sloping straight line.
*   **Shifted Supply Curve ($S'$):** An upward-sloping straight line located above and to the left of the original supply curve $S$.

### 3. Key Intersections and Coordinates
*   **Initial Equilibrium ($E_1$):** The intersection of Demand curve ($D$) and original Supply curve ($S$).
    *   Quantity coordinate: **40** on the $Q$-axis.
    *   Price coordinate: **18** on the $P$-axis.
    *   Dotted lines connect this intersection to $40$ on the $Q$-axis and $18$ on the $P$-axis.
*   **New Equilibrium ($E_2$):** The intersection of Demand curve ($D$) and shifted Supply curve ($S'$).
    *   Quantity coordinate: **30** on the $Q$-axis.
    *   Price coordinate: **22** on the $P$-axis.
    *   Dotted lines connect this intersection to $30$ on the $Q$-axis and $22$ on the $P$-axis.
*   **Additional Point:** There is a dotted horizontal line originating from the shifted Supply curve ($S'$) that intersects the vertical $P$-axis at the value **17**. This point corresponds to the $Q$-axis position of $30$.

### 4. Numerical Values on Axes
*   **$P$-axis:** Marked values are **17**, **18**, **22**, and **50**.
*   **$Q$-axis:** Marked values are **30** and **40**.

### 5. Spatial Geometry
*   The lines $S$ and $S'$ are parallel.
*   The dotted lines are strictly vertical (connecting the curves to the $Q$-axis) or strictly horizontal (connecting the curves to the $P$-axis).
*   The overall layout shows a decrease in quantity (from 40 to 30) and an increase in price (from 18 to 22) as a result of the supply shift from $S$ to $S'$.
